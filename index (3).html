    Know your connection.

    Chat is encrypted on your device with AES-256-GCM using an ephemeral P-256 ECDH key exchange, then sent over an encrypted WebRTC data channel. Only the paired devices hold the chat key.

    Verify the person, not a profile.

    Compare this full SHA-512 fingerprint with your friend through a trusted call or in person. If it differs, disconnect. Short room codes alone do not authenticate a person. 

    Pair first to generate your fingerprint.

    What privacy means here

    • No names, email addresses, accounts, or stored chat history.
    • Supabase temporarily stores room capabilities and connection setup—not chat or songs. Setup is deleted after both devices confirm connection. Unfinished rooms expire after 10 minutes; a cleanup job removes idle expired records. Hosting providers can retain network logs.
    • Direct WebRTC can reveal your IP address to your peer. This is private messaging, not IP anonymity.
    • Music files use the encrypted WebRTC channel. Anyone you send content to can save it.
    • A TURN provider, if configured, relays encrypted traffic when a direct path fails. It cannot decrypt the WebRTC content.

    512 bits describes this verification hash, not “512-bit encryption.” There is no invented 516-bit security claim. This implementation has not had an independent security audit.
